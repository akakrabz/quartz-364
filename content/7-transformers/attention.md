*Lecture 19 · [[7-transformers/index|Transformers]] · builds on [[7-transformers/text-encodings-and-embeddings|embeddings]], [[1-foundations/linear-algebra-in-pytorch|matrix products]] and [[3-linear-models/multiclass-logistic-regression|softmax]] · motivated by [[7-transformers/sequence-to-sequence-rnns|the limits of seq2seq RNNs]]*

> [!abstract] In one breath
> Self-attention lets every token build its new representation as a **weighted average of all tokens' value vectors**, with weights decided by how well its **query** matches each token's **key**. From token embeddings $Z\in\mathbb R^{N\times d_{emb}}$: $Q = ZW_Q$, $K = ZW_K$, $V = ZW_V$, and
> $$\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt H}\Big)V,$$
> where the softmax runs along each row so every token's weights sum to 1, and $1/\sqrt H$ keeps the scores from saturating the softmax. **Masked** self-attention adds $-\infty$ above the diagonal so a decoder position cannot look at later words; **cross-attention** takes its queries from the decoder and its keys and values from the encoder. All $N$ outputs come from a few matrix products — no step-by-step recurrence — at the price of an $N\times N$ weight matrix.

## What it is

The Transformer of [[7-transformers/attention-is-all-you-need|"Attention Is All You Need"]] replaces recurrence with attention. Lecture 19 introduces the three variants it needs — self-attention, masked self-attention and cross-attention — inside a stripped-down "Skinny Transformer" (see [[7-transformers/transformer-architecture|the architecture page]]).

> [!definition] Definition — scaled dot-product attention
> Let $Z\in\mathbb R^{N\times d_{emb}}$ hold the embeddings of $N$ tokens, one per row. With learned matrices $W_Q, W_K\in\mathbb R^{d_{emb}\times H}$ and $W_V\in\mathbb R^{d_{emb}\times d_{emb}}$,
> $$
> Q = ZW_Q\in\mathbb R^{N\times H},\qquad K = ZW_K\in\mathbb R^{N\times H},\qquad V = ZW_V\in\mathbb R^{N\times d_{emb}},
> $$
> $$
> A = \text{softmax}_{\text{row}}\Big(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt H}\Big)\in\mathbb R^{N\times N},\qquad O = \text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = AV\in\mathbb R^{N\times d_{emb}}.
> $$
> Row $i$ of $Q$ is token $i$'s **query** $q_i$, row $j$ of $K$ its **key** $k_j$, row $j$ of $V$ its **value** $v_j$; $A$ holds the **attention weights**. (The lecture writes $H$ for the query/key dimension; the paper writes $d_k$, and $d_v$ for the value dimension, which the lecture sets to $d_{emb}$.)

## What you should know

- How $Q, K, V$ are made from the input by three separate linear maps, and their shapes.
- The formula, and that $s_{ij} = q_i^\top k_j$: entry $(i,j)$ of $QK^\top$ is how much token $i$'s query matches token $j$'s key.
- The softmax runs over **each row** (over keys $j$), so each row of $A$ is a probability distribution; output row $o_i = \sum_j a_{ij}v_j$ is a weighted average of the value rows.
- Why divide by $\sqrt H$: for random unit-variance entries $q^\top k$ has variance $H$, and large scores push the softmax to one-hot, where its gradients vanish.
- Masked self-attention: the mask $M_{ij} = 0$ for $j\le i$, $-\infty$ for $j > i$; it makes position $i$ blind to later positions so that a whole teacher-forced target sentence can be trained in **one** pass.
- Cross-attention: queries from the decoder ($d_{out}$ of them), keys and values from the encoder ($d_{in}$ of them), so $A$ is $d_{out}\times d_{in}$.
- Self-attention without positional information is **permutation equivariant** — shuffling the input tokens just shuffles the outputs — hence [[7-transformers/positional-encoding|positional encoding]].
- Cost: $O(N^2)$ attention weights per head, but no sequential dependence between positions.

## Queries, keys and values

Think of each token publishing a **key** ("what I contain") and a **value** ("what I will hand over"), and asking a **query** ("what I am looking for"). The dot product $q_i^\top k_j$ is large when token $i$'s question matches token $j$'s description, near zero when they are unrelated, and negative when they disagree. Because $W_Q$ and $W_K$ are different matrices, "what $i$ looks for" and "what $i$ offers" are decoupled: $s_{ij}\ne s_{ji}$ in general.

| Quantity | Shape | Made by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $Z$ (embedded tokens) | $N\times d_{emb}$ | `nn.Embedding` |
| $Q$, $K$ | $N\times H$ | `nn.Linear(d_emb, H)` each |
| $V$ | $N\times d_{emb}$ | `nn.Linear(d_emb, d_emb)` |
| $S = QK^\top$, $A$ | $N\times N$ | matrix product, softmax |
| $O = AV$ | $N\times d_{emb}$ | matrix product |

The output has one row per token and the same width as the input, so attention layers can be stacked and added to their input ([[7-transformers/layer-norm-and-feed-forward|residual connections]]). The lecture's code uses `nn.Linear` layers, which also add a bias to each projection; the formulas above leave biases out.

## Scaled dot-product attention, step by step

For $N = 4$ tokens, $S = QK^\top$ is the table of all query–key dot products:

$$
S = QK^\top = \begin{bmatrix} q_1^\top k_1 & q_1^\top k_2 & q_1^\top k_3 & q_1^\top k_4\\ q_2^\top k_1 & q_2^\top k_2 & q_2^\top k_3 & q_2^\top k_4\\ q_3^\top k_1 & q_3^\top k_2 & q_3^\top k_3 & q_3^\top k_4\\ q_4^\top k_1 & q_4^\top k_2 & q_4^\top k_3 & q_4^\top k_4\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Row $i$ says how strongly token $i$ should attend to each token $j$. Dividing by $\sqrt H$ and applying the softmax along each row turns the scores into weights that are positive and sum to one; the lecture's illustration:

$$
A = \text{softmax}\Big(\frac{S}{\sqrt H}\Big) = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 & 0.2 & 0.1 & 0.1\\ 0.2 & 0.4 & 0.3 & 0.1\\ 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.5 & 0.3\\ 0.3 & 0.1 & 0.3 & 0.3\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Finally $O = AV$. With $d_{emb} = 2$, the first output row is

$$
o_1 = \big[\,a_{11}v_{11} + a_{12}v_{21} + a_{13}v_{31} + a_{14}v_{41},\;\; a_{11}v_{12} + a_{12}v_{22} + a_{13}v_{32} + a_{14}v_{42}\,\big] = \sum_{j=1}^{4} a_{1j}\,v_j .
$$

Every entry of $o_1$ uses the **same** four weights $a_{11},\dots,a_{14}$: $a_{13}$ is how much of token 3's value goes into token 1's output. Each output row is a convex combination of the value rows.

### Why divide by √H

The paper's argument (a footnote in its Section 3.2.1): if the entries of $q$ and $k$ are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, then $q^\top k = \sum_{m=1}^{H} q_m k_m$ has mean 0 and variance $H$. With $H = 512$ the scores have a standard deviation of about 23, the softmax puts almost all weight on one key, and the gradient through a saturated softmax is nearly zero. Dividing by $\sqrt H$ restores unit variance at every $H$.

## A worked example

Three tokens with $d_{emb} = H = 2$, no biases:

$$
Z = \begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix},\quad W_Q = \begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad W_K = \begin{bmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{bmatrix},\quad W_V = \begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}.
$$

**1. Project.**

$$
Q = ZW_Q = \begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix},\qquad K = ZW_K = \begin{bmatrix}1&0\\1&1\\2&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad V = ZW_V = \begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&2\\1&2\end{bmatrix}.
$$

**2. Scores and scaling.** $s_{ij} = q_i^\top k_j$, e.g. $s_{13} = 1\cdot2 + 1\cdot1 = 3$:

$$
S = QK^\top = \begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\\0&1&1\\1&3&4\end{bmatrix},\qquad \frac{S}{\sqrt2} = \begin{bmatrix}0.707&1.414&2.121\\0&0.707&0.707\\0.707&2.121&2.828\end{bmatrix}.
$$

**3. Row softmax.** Row 1: $e^{0.707}, e^{1.414}, e^{2.121} = 2.028, 4.113, 8.342$, sum $14.484$, so $(0.140, 0.284, 0.576)$:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix}0.140&0.284&0.576\\0.198&0.401&0.401\\0.074&0.306&0.620\end{bmatrix}\qquad(\text{each row sums to }1).
$$

**4. Weighted sum of values.** $o_1 = 0.140\,(1,0) + 0.284\,(0,2) + 0.576\,(1,2) = (0.716,\ 1.720)$:

$$
O = AV = \begin{bmatrix}0.716&1.720\\0.599&1.604\\0.694&1.851\end{bmatrix}.
$$

## Masked self-attention

The decoder's first attention layer reads the target sentence, shifted right behind `<SOS>` ([[7-transformers/sequence-to-sequence-rnns#teacher-forcing-and-lining-up-the-loss|teacher forcing]]). If position $i$ could attend to position $i+1$, it would see the very word it must predict and learn to copy it. The fix is an additive mask:

$$
\text{MaskedAttention}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\Big(\frac{QK^\top + M}{\sqrt H}\Big)V,\qquad M_{ij} = \begin{cases}0 & i\ge j\\ -\infty & i < j.\end{cases}
$$

Rows index queries and columns index keys, so the $-\infty$ entries above the diagonal are "query $i$ looking at a later key $j$"; $e^{-\infty} = 0$, so those weights become exactly zero and each row renormalizes over the positions up to and including itself. For $N = 5$:

$$
M = \begin{bmatrix}0&-\infty&-\infty&-\infty&-\infty\\0&0&-\infty&-\infty&-\infty\\0&0&0&-\infty&-\infty\\0&0&0&0&-\infty\\0&0&0&0&0\end{bmatrix}.
$$

In the worked example, masking changes the first two rows: row 1 becomes $(1, 0, 0)$ and row 2 becomes $\text{softmax}(0, 0.707) = (0.330, 0.670, 0)$, so $o_1 = (1, 0)$ and $o_2 = 0.330\,(1,0) + 0.670\,(0,2) = (0.330, 1.340)$; row 3 is unchanged. Where the $-\infty$ is added — before or after dividing by $\sqrt H$ — makes no difference, since $-\infty/\sqrt H = -\infty$ and $0/\sqrt H = 0$ (the lecture's formula adds it before, the code after).

This is what removes the first [[7-transformers/sequence-to-sequence-rnns#three-limitations|limitation of seq2seq RNNs]]: for the target "The dog is cute!", the decoder produces the next-word predictions for `<SOS>`, `<SOS> The`, `<SOS> The dog`, …, `<SOS> The dog is cute !` all at once, each row seeing only its own prefix. At inference the model still generates one word at a time, appending each prediction to the input.

The lecture builds the mask with [`torch.triu`](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.triu.html), which keeps the entries on and above the given diagonal:

```python
n_tokens = Q_1.size(0)
if n_tokens > 1:
    causal_mask = torch.zeros((n_tokens, n_tokens))
    inf_values = torch.zeros((n_tokens, n_tokens)).fill_(float('-inf'))
    causal_mask += torch.triu(inf_values, diagonal=1)   # -inf strictly above diagonal
    attn_output_1 = self.masked_attn_head(Q_1, K_1, V_1, mask=causal_mask.to(DEVICE))
else:
    attn_output_1 = self.masked_attn_head(Q_1, K_1, V_1)
```

## Cross-attention

Cross-attention connects the two halves. With $E\in\mathbb R^{d_{in}\times d_{emb}}$ the encoder output (one row per source word) and $D\in\mathbb R^{d_{out}\times d_{emb}}$ the decoder's masked self-attention output (one row per target position),

$$
Q_{dec} = DW_Q,\quad K_{enc} = EW_K,\quad V_{enc} = EW_V,\qquad \text{CrossAttention} = \text{softmax}\Big(\frac{Q_{dec}K_{enc}^\top}{\sqrt H}\Big)V_{enc}.
$$

| Tensor | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| $Q_{dec}$ | $d_{out}\times H$ |
| $K_{enc}$ | $d_{in}\times H$ |
| $V_{enc}$ | $d_{in}\times d_{emb}$ |
| attention weights | $d_{out}\times d_{in}$ — rectangular |
| output | $d_{out}\times d_{emb}$ — one row per **decoder** position |

Each target position asks "which source words matter for me now?" and receives a weighted average of the encoder's representations. No mask is needed: the whole source sentence is known. Unlike the single context vector of an S2S RNN, the encoder output is consulted afresh at every decoder position and is never overwritten. The lecture's example instantiation (English 7 tokens, French 9 tokens, $d_{emb} = 32$, $H = 16$) prints an encoder output of shape $(7, 32)$ and decoder scores of shape $(9, 560)$ — 560 being the French vocabulary — so its cross-attention weights are $9\times 7$.

![[l19-encoder-decoder-transformer.gif|Animation: the encoder processes "I arrived at the …" in parallel, then the decoder generates the French sentence word by word, attending to all encoder outputs]]

The animation the lecture borrows from the TensorFlow Transformer tutorial shows the two phases: all source words are encoded at once, then decoding starts from `<start>` and adds one French word at a time, each new step reading every encoder output.

## The lecture's code

`ScaledDotProductAttention` is the formula for unbatched inputs (one sentence, tokens along dimension 0):

```python
class ScaledDotProductAttention(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self):
        super(ScaledDotProductAttention, self).__init__()

    def forward(self, Q, K, V, mask=None):
        # shapes: Q,K (n,H); V (n,d_emb)
        d_k = K.size(1)
        dot_product = Q@K.T
        scaled_dot_product = dot_product/np.sqrt(d_k)
        if mask is not None:
            attn_weights = torch.softmax(scaled_dot_product + mask, dim=1)
        else:
            attn_weights = torch.softmax(scaled_dot_product, dim=1)
        outputs = attn_weights@V
        return outputs
```

`dim=1` is the key dimension of the $(n, n)$ score matrix: each row is normalized. The Skinny Transformer's encoder is an embedding, three projections and one call:

```python
class EncoderAttention(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, num_embeddings, embedding_dim, hidden_dim):
        super(EncoderAttention, self).__init__()
        self.embedding = nn.Embedding(num_embeddings, embedding_dim)
        self.fc_Q = nn.Linear(embedding_dim, hidden_dim)
        self.fc_K = nn.Linear(embedding_dim, hidden_dim)
        self.fc_V = nn.Linear(embedding_dim, embedding_dim)
        self.attn_head = ScaledDotProductAttention()
        self.activation = nn.ReLU()

    def forward(self, x):
        z = self.embedding(x).squeeze(1)       # (n_tokens, d_emb)
        Q, K, V = self.fc_Q(z), self.fc_K(z), self.fc_V(z)
        return self.activation(self.attn_head(Q, K, V))
```

In the decoder, the cross-attention queries come from the (ReLU of the) masked attention output and the keys and values from the encoder output `z_enc`:

```python
Q_2 = self.fc_Q_2(self.activation(attn_output_1))   # queries: decoder
K_2 = self.fc_K_2(z_enc)                            # keys: encoder output
V_2 = self.fc_V_2(z_enc)                            # values: encoder output
attn_output_2 = self.activation(self.cross_attn_head(Q_2, K_2, V_2))
```

How these blocks are assembled, trained and decoded — and how they compare with the seq2seq RNNs — is on [[7-transformers/transformer-architecture|the architecture page]].

## Order does not matter to self-attention

Permute the input tokens with a permutation matrix $P$: $Z' = PZ$. Then $Q' = PQ$, $K' = PK$, $V' = PV$ and

$$
Q'K'^\top = P\,QK^\top P^\top .
$$

Permuting rows and columns of a matrix permutes the rows and the entries within each row of its row-softmax in the same way, so $A' = PAP^\top$, and

$$
O' = A'V' = PAP^\top P V = PAV = PO .
$$

The outputs are the old outputs, reordered the same way: self-attention is **permutation equivariant**. Each token sees the others only as an unordered set — "dog bites man" and "man bites dog" produce the same three output vectors. An RNN knows word order from its recurrence; a Transformer must be told it, which is what [[7-transformers/positional-encoding|positional encoding]] does. (The causal mask does break the symmetry partly, since it depends on positions, but it says only which tokens came earlier, not in what order.)

## Cost

| | self-attention | recurrent layer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| work per layer | $O(N^2\cdot H)$ for $QK^\top$ (and $O(N^2 d_{emb})$ for $AV$) | $O(N\cdot H^2)$ |
| sequential steps | $O(1)$ — a few matrix products | $O(N)$ — one per token |
| path between two tokens | 1 step | up to $N$ steps |
| memory for weights | the $N\times N$ matrix $A$ | — |

(The first three rows are the comparison in Table 1 of [[7-transformers/attention-is-all-you-need|the paper]].) For sentences of 25 tokens the $N^2$ term is tiny; for very long inputs (documents, images cut into many patches) it becomes the main cost.

## In PyTorch

Batched code puts the token dimension second to last, so the transpose must swap only the last two dimensions:

```python
scores = Q @ K.transpose(-2, -1) / math.sqrt(Q.size(-1))   # (..., N, N)
A = torch.softmax(scores, dim=-1)                          # rows sum to 1
O = A @ V
O2 = F.scaled_dot_product_attention(Q, K, V)               # same result
O3 = F.scaled_dot_product_attention(Q, K, V, is_causal=True)  # causal mask
```

[`torch.nn.functional.scaled_dot_product_attention`](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.nn.functional.scaled_dot_product_attention.html) computes the whole formula, with the same $1/\sqrt{\text{last dim of }Q}$ scale by default, `is_causal=True` for the decoder mask, or an `attn_mask` (a float mask is added to the scores; a boolean mask marks with `True` the positions that **may** be attended to) — and it can use fused, memory-efficient kernels. [`nn.MultiheadAttention`](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.nn.MultiheadAttention.html) adds the projections and several heads; see [[7-transformers/multi-head-attention|multi-head attention]].

> [!trap] Common mistakes
> - **The mask's direction.** Earlier positions must not attend to **later** ones: the $-\infty$ entries are above the diagonal (query $i$, key $j > i$). The notebook's sentence "we should not allow future words to attend to earlier words" states it backwards — future words attending to earlier words is exactly what is allowed.
> - **Softmax over the wrong dimension.** Normalize over keys (the last dimension of $QK^\top$), so each **row** sums to 1. A column softmax mixes different queries' scores.
> - **`.T` on batched tensors.** `K.T` is fine for the lecture's 2-D tensors, but on a 3-D tensor it reverses **all** dimensions; use `K.transpose(-2, -1)`.
> - **Forgetting the scale.** Without $1/\sqrt H$, training can stall as the softmax saturates (the bigger $H$, the worse).
> - **A fully masked row.** If every key of some query is $-\infty$, `torch.softmax` returns `NaN` for that row ($0/0$). The causal mask never does this — the diagonal is always allowed — but padding masks can.
> - **Masking cross-attention causally.** The source sentence is fully known; cross-attention needs no causal mask.
> - **Thinking attention knows word order.** Without positional information it does not (see above).

> [!question]- Check yourself
> 1. A decoder has 9 target positions, the encoder 7 source tokens, $d_{emb} = 32$, $H = 16$. Give the shapes of the masked self-attention weights, the cross-attention weights and the cross-attention output.
> 2. In the worked example, all three keys are replaced by the same vector $k$. What is $O$?
> 3. Recompute the masked row 2 of the worked example from $S/\sqrt2$.
> 4. At inference the Skinny decoder is re-run on the growing prefix and only its last row's prediction is used. Would that prediction change if the causal mask were dropped? Would it in a decoder with two stacked layers?
>
> > [!solution]- Answers
> > 1. Masked self-attention weights $9\times9$; cross-attention weights $9\times7$; cross-attention output $9\times32$.
> > 2. Every row of $S$ is constant ($s_{ij} = q_i^\top k$ for all $j$), so every row of $A$ is $(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$ and every output row is the mean value $(\tfrac23, \tfrac43)$: attention degenerates to averaging.
> > 3. Row 2 of $S/\sqrt2$ is $(0, 0.707, 0.707)$; masking the third entry leaves $\text{softmax}(0, 0.707) = (1, 2.028)/3.028 = (0.330, 0.670)$, and $o_2 = (0.330, 1.340)$.
> > 4. Not in the one-layer Skinny decoder: the last row may attend to every position with or without the mask, and the other rows never feed into it. With two layers it would: layer 1's earlier rows become layer 2's keys and values, and without the mask they would have looked at later words — different from what the model saw in training. Applying the mask always keeps inference consistent with training.
