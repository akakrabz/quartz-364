> [!abstract] In one breath
> A computational graph is a DAG: nodes hold intermediate values, arrows say which values feed which operation. **Backpropagation** is one forward pass (compute and store every node) plus one backward pass that sends adjoints $\bar z_i=\partial f/\partial z_i$ from the seed ($\bar z_{\text{seed}}=1$) back to the inputs. Every exam asks the same three things — node values, local partials, adjoints — and the points are lost at nodes with **several outgoing arrows**, whose adjoint is a **sum**.

## Reading the exam figures

The label on an arrow leaving a node is **that node's own value**, written in terms of its inputs. In Fall 2025 the arrow $a_2\to a_5$ is labelled $a_1^2$, so $a_2=a_1^2$; the arrow out of $a_6$ is labelled $a_3+a_4+a_5$, so $a_6=a_3+a_4+a_5$. Every arrow leaving the same node carries the same label. Fall 2024, Spring 2025 and HW2 use the same convention.

$$
\bar z_i=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_i}=\sum_{j\in\text{successors}(i)}\bar z_j\,\frac{\partial z_j}{\partial z_i},\qquad \bar z_{\text{seed}}=1.
$$

## Local partials

| node | local partials | node | local partials |
|---|---|---|---|
| $z=u+v$ | $1,\;1$ | $z=u^n$ | $nu^{n-1}$ |
| $z=u-v$ | $1,\;-1$ | $z=e^u$ | $e^u=z$ |
| $z=uv$ | $v$ (w.r.t. $u$), $u$ (w.r.t. $v$) | $z=\ln u$ | $1/u$ |
| $z=u/v$ | $\dfrac1v,\;-\dfrac{u}{v^2}$ | $z=\sin u$, $z=\cos u$ | $\cos u$, $-\sin u$ |
| $z=cu$, $z=u+c$ | $c$, $1$ | $z=\sigma(u)$ | $z(1-z)$ |

> [!recipe] Recipe for parts (a)–(d)
> 1. **Node values.** Read each node off its outgoing label, then substitute back to $x$.
> 2. **Local partials.** One per arrow, $\partial(\text{child})/\partial(\text{parent})$, holding the child's other inputs fixed. Leave them in node variables.
> 3. **Adjoints.** Seed $=1$, then right to left. A node with several outgoing arrows sums one term per arrow.
> 4. **Check.** The input's adjoint must equal $df/dx$ from part (a). Not asked on the exams, but it catches every dropped term.

> [!example]- HW2 Q2 — softmax $g=\dfrac{e^x}{e^x+e^y+e^v}$
> $z_4=e^{z_1}$, $z_5=e^{z_2}$, $z_6=e^{z_3}$, $z_7=z_4+z_5+z_6$, $z_8=z_4/z_7$. The division has local partials $\partial z_8/\partial z_4=1/z_7$ and $\partial z_8/\partial z_7=-z_4/z_7^2$.
> $$
> \bar z_8=1,\quad \bar z_7=-\frac{z_4}{z_7^2},\quad \bar z_6=\bar z_5=-\frac{z_4}{z_7^2},\quad \bar z_4=\frac1{z_7}-\frac{z_4}{z_7^2}=\frac{z_5+z_6}{z_7^2},
> $$
> $$
> \bar z_3=-\frac{z_4z_6}{z_7^2},\quad \bar z_2=-\frac{z_4z_5}{z_7^2},\quad \bar z_1=\bar z_4z_4=\frac{z_4(z_5+z_6)}{z_7^2}=s(1-s)\ \text{ with } s=g.
> $$
> $z_4$ has two outgoing arrows (into $z_7$ and $z_8$), so $\bar z_4$ has two terms.

> [!example]- HW2 Q3 — stacked sigmoids, vanishing gradients, a skip connection
> $z_2=z_1-a$, $z_3=\sigma(z_2)$, $z_4=z_3-b$, $z_5=\sigma(z_4)$, $z_6=z_5-c$, $z_7=\sigma(z_6)=f$.
> $$
> \frac{\partial f}{\partial c}=-z_7(1-z_7),\quad \frac{\partial f}{\partial b}=-z_7(1-z_7)\,z_5(1-z_5),\quad \frac{\partial f}{\partial a}=-z_7(1-z_7)\,z_5(1-z_5)\,z_3(1-z_3).
> $$
> With $x=a=1$ and $b=c=\tfrac12$: $z=(1,0,\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12)$, so $\partial f/\partial c=-\tfrac14$, $\partial f/\partial b=-\tfrac1{16}$, $\partial f/\partial a=-\tfrac1{64}$. Each sigmoid multiplies the gradient by $\sigma'\le\tfrac14$, so it shrinks as it travels back: a **vanishing gradient**.
>
> Skip connection $g=\sigma(z_6)+z_2$: $\partial g/\partial c$ and $\partial g/\partial b$ are unchanged, but $\partial g/\partial a=-(1+\tfrac1{64})=-\tfrac{65}{64}$. The skip path hands the gradient straight back without the $\tfrac14$ factors — the idea behind [[5-deep-networks/residual-connections|residual connections]].

## Practice: the Lecture 12 review graph

$f(x,y)=2\cos^2(xy)+\ln(\cos(xy))$. Find each node's value, the local partials and all adjoints, and check $\bar z_1,\bar z_2$ against $\partial f/\partial x$, $\partial f/\partial y$.

> [!solution]- Solution (checked numerically)
> $$
> z_3=z_1z_2=xy,\quad z_4=\cos z_3,\quad z_5=z_4^2,\quad z_6=2z_5,\quad z_7=\ln z_4,\quad z_8=z_6+z_7.
> $$
> Local partials: $\dfrac{\partial z_8}{\partial z_6}=\dfrac{\partial z_8}{\partial z_7}=1$, $\dfrac{\partial z_6}{\partial z_5}=2$, $\dfrac{\partial z_5}{\partial z_4}=2z_4$, $\dfrac{\partial z_7}{\partial z_4}=\dfrac1{z_4}$, $\dfrac{\partial z_4}{\partial z_3}=-\sin z_3$, $\dfrac{\partial z_3}{\partial z_1}=z_2$, $\dfrac{\partial z_3}{\partial z_2}=z_1$.
> $$
> \bar z_8=\bar z_7=\bar z_6=1,\quad \bar z_5=2,\quad \bar z_4=\bar z_5(2z_4)+\bar z_7\frac1{z_4}=4z_4+\frac1{z_4},\quad \bar z_3=-\sin(z_3)\Big(4z_4+\frac1{z_4}\Big),
> $$
> $$
> \bar z_1=z_2\bar z_3=-y\sin(xy)\Big(4\cos(xy)+\frac1{\cos(xy)}\Big),\qquad \bar z_2=z_1\bar z_3=-x\sin(xy)\Big(4\cos(xy)+\frac1{\cos(xy)}\Big).
> $$
> Direct check: $\partial f/\partial x=-4y\sin(xy)\cos(xy)-y\tan(xy)$, the same thing. (Numerically at $x=0.3$, $y=0.5$: both $-0.3711$.)
