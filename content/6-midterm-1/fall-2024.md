## Problem 1 — Slicing and a boolean mask (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 1
> ```python
> import torch
> a = torch.randn((8, 8))
> b = a[::2, ::4]
> d = ???
> c = a[d]
> ```
> **(a)** What will be the shape of `b`? (Circle one.) i. `(8, 8)` ii. `(4, 2)` iii. `(2, 4)` iv. `(8,)`
>
> **(b)** Suppose we would like `c` to contain all values of `a` that have a magnitude greater than $\tfrac12$. Write the line of code for `d`.

> [!solution]- Solution
> **(a) ii. `(4, 2)`.** `::2` on the rows keeps rows 0, 2, 4, 6 (four rows); `::4` on the columns keeps columns 0 and 4 (two columns). The stop is the end of the axis, and the step counts from 0.
>
> **(b)** `d = torch.abs(a) > 0.5`
>
> The official key also accepts the two-sided version `d = (a > 0.5) | (a < -0.5)`. Either way `d` is a boolean tensor of shape `(8, 8)` and `c = a[d]` is **1-D**, with the selected entries in row-major order.

## Problem 2 — List indexing, views and a matrix product (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 2
> ```python
> import torch
> x = torch.tensor([[1, -2, 3, -4], [-5, 6, 6, 5], [4, 3, 2, 1]])
> y = x[:, [0, 2, 3]]
> z = y.T @ x
> ```
> **(a)** State the resulting tensor `y`.
>
> **(b)** (True/False) The tensor `y` is a view of tensor `x`, i.e. they share data pointers.
>
> **(c)** Determine the values in tensor `z`, if the code successfully runs. If not, explain the error.

> [!solution]- Solution
> **(a)** Columns 0, 2 and 3 of `x`:
> $$
> y=\begin{bmatrix}1&3&-4\\-5&6&5\\4&2&1\end{bmatrix}.
> $$
> **(b) False.** Indexing with a *list* (advanced indexing) allocates a new tensor, so `x` and `y` have different data pointers. Only basic slicing (`start:stop:step`, integers) returns a view.
>
> **(c)** It runs: `y.T` is $3\times3$ and `x` is $3\times4$, so `z` is $3\times4$.
> $$
> z=y^\top x=\begin{bmatrix}1&-5&4\\3&6&2\\-4&5&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&-2&3&-4\\-5&6&6&5\\4&3&2&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}42&-20&-19&-25\\-19&36&49&20\\-25&41&20&42\end{bmatrix}.
> $$
> Spot-check one entry: $z_{11}=1\cdot1+(-5)(-5)+4\cdot4=42$.

## Problem 3 — Gradient descent by hand (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 3
> Consider $f(x,y)=(x+3y)^2+(x-2)^2$.
>
> **(a)** Determine the gradient $\nabla f(x,y)$.
>
> **(b)** Let the initial point at iteration $k=0$ be $(x^{(0)},y^{(0)})=(0,0)$ and the step size $\alpha=\tfrac18$. Apply gradient descent to obtain the iterates at $k=1$ and $2$.

> [!solution]- Solution
> **(a)**
> $$
> \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=2(x+3y)+2(x-2),\qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=6(x+3y),\qquad
> \nabla f(x,y)=\begin{bmatrix}2(x+3y)+2(x-2)\\6(x+3y)\end{bmatrix}.
> $$
> **(b)** The update is $(x^{(k+1)},y^{(k+1)})=(x^{(k)},y^{(k)})-\alpha\,\nabla f(x^{(k)},y^{(k)})$.
>
> $k=1$: $\nabla f(0,0)=(0+2(-2),\,0)=(-4,0)$, so $(x^{(1)},y^{(1)})=(0,0)-\tfrac18(-4,0)=(\tfrac12,\,0)$.
>
> $k=2$: $\nabla f(\tfrac12,0)=\big(2\cdot\tfrac12+2(\tfrac12-2),\;6\cdot\tfrac12\big)=(-2,\,3)$, so
> $$
> (x^{(2)},y^{(2)})=\left(\tfrac12+\tfrac14,\;0-\tfrac38\right)=\left(\tfrac34,\,-\tfrac38\right).
> $$
> (The official key writes the update with $x^{(k+1)}$ on both sides; the right-hand side is the *current* iterate, see [[6-midterm-1/errata|errata]].)

> [!question] Problem 4
> Consider the computational graph above, where $f(x)=w_6$.
>
> **(a)** Determine the function $f(x)$ represented by the graph.
>
> **(b)** Determine the partial derivatives of each successor node with respect to its predecessors, e.g. $\partial w_6/\partial w_5$, $\partial w_6/\partial w_4$, $\partial w_6/\partial w_2$, etc.
>
> **(c)** Determine the adjoints at each node $\bar w_i=\partial f/\partial w_i$.

> [!solution]- Solution
> **(a)** Read each node off the label on the arrows *leaving* it:
> $$
> w_2=\sin w_1=\sin x,\quad w_3=w_2^2=\sin^2x,\quad w_4=e^{w_2}=e^{\sin x},\quad w_5=w_3+364=\sin^2x+364,
> $$
> $$
> f(x)=w_6=w_2+w_4+w_5=\sin x+e^{\sin x}+\sin^2x+364.
> $$
> **(b)** One local partial per arrow:
>
> | arrow | local partial | arrow | local partial |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | $w_5\to w_6$ | $\partial w_6/\partial w_5=1$ | $w_2\to w_3$ | $\partial w_3/\partial w_2=2w_2$ |
> | $w_4\to w_6$ | $\partial w_6/\partial w_4=1$ | $w_2\to w_4$ | $\partial w_4/\partial w_2=e^{w_2}$ |
> | $w_2\to w_6$ | $\partial w_6/\partial w_2=1$ | $w_1\to w_2$ | $\partial w_2/\partial w_1=\cos w_1$ |
> | $w_3\to w_5$ | $\partial w_5/\partial w_3=1$ | | |
>
> **(c)** Seed $\bar w_6=1$, then right to left; $w_2$ has **three** outgoing arrows, so it sums three terms:
> $$
> \bar w_6=1,\quad \bar w_5=\bar w_6\cdot1=1,\quad \bar w_4=\bar w_6\cdot1=1,\quad \bar w_3=\bar w_5\cdot1=1,
> $$
> $$
> \bar w_2=\bar w_3\,(2w_2)+\bar w_4\,e^{w_2}+\bar w_6\cdot1=2w_2+e^{w_2}+1,\qquad
> \bar w_1=\bar w_2\cos w_1=\cos(w_1)\,\big(2w_2+e^{w_2}+1\big).
> $$
> **Check:** with $w_1=x$, $w_2=\sin x$ this is $\cos x\,(2\sin x+e^{\sin x}+1)=f'(x)$. (Numerically at $x=0.3$: both give $2.8038$.)

## Problem 5 — Weighted linear regression (35 pts)

> [!question] Problem 5
> Consider a variation on linear regression where we assign a different weight or significance value $\sigma=\{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_N\}\in\mathbb R^N$ to each point in the dataset $\mathcal D=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^N$. The objective for this weighted linear regression is
> $$
> \min_{w_1,w_0} f(w_1,w_0)=\min_{w_1,w_0}\frac12\sum_{i=1}^N\big[\sigma_i\,(y_i-w_1x_i-w_0)\big]^2. \tag{1}
> $$
> **(a)** Determine $\partial f/\partial w_1$ and $\partial f/\partial w_0$.
>
> Let $\Sigma\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}$ be the diagonal matrix of the $\sigma_i$. Eq. (1) can be written in vector form, with $w=(w_1,w_0)^\top$, as
> $$
> \min_w J(w)=\min_w\frac12\big\Vert\Sigma(Xw-y)\big\Vert_2^2. \tag{2}
> $$
> **(b)** Let $N=3$. Write the matrix $X$ in terms of $x_1,x_2,x_3$.
>
> **(c)** Determine $\nabla_wJ(w)$. *Hint: consider the substitutions $Z=\Sigma X$ and $v=\Sigma y$.*
>
> **(d)** Determine a closed-form solution, i.e. the minimizing $w$, by setting your answer from (c) to zero.
>
> **(e)** What is the closed-form solution when each $\sigma_i=1$?

> [!solution]- Solution
> **(a)** Chain rule on each square; the inner derivative is $-\sigma_ix_i$ for $w_1$ and $-\sigma_i$ for $w_0$:
> $$
> \frac{\partial f}{\partial w_1}=-\sum_{i=1}^N\sigma_i^2\,x_i\,(y_i-w_1x_i-w_0),\qquad
> \frac{\partial f}{\partial w_0}=-\sum_{i=1}^N\sigma_i^2\,(y_i-w_1x_i-w_0).
> $$
> **(b)** One row per point, with a column of ones for the bias $w_0$ (second entry of $w$):
> $$
> X=\begin{bmatrix}x_1&1\\x_2&1\\x_3&1\end{bmatrix}.
> $$
> **(c)** With $Z=\Sigma X$ and $v=\Sigma y$, $J(w)=\tfrac12\Vert Zw-v\Vert_2^2$ is ordinary least squares, whose gradient is $Z^\top(Zw-v)$:
> $$
> \nabla_wJ=Z^\top(Zw-v)=X^\top\Sigma^\top(\Sigma Xw-\Sigma y)=X^\top\Sigma^\top\Sigma Xw-X^\top\Sigma^\top\Sigma y.
> $$
> **(d)** Set it to zero:
> $$
> X^\top\Sigma^\top\Sigma X\,w=X^\top\Sigma^\top\Sigma y\quad\Longrightarrow\quad w^*=\big(X^\top\Sigma^\top\Sigma X\big)^{-1}X^\top\Sigma^\top\Sigma\,y.
> $$
> **(e)** All $\sigma_i=1$ means $\Sigma=I$, so $w^*=(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y$ — ordinary linear regression (the normal equations). Equivalently: with $\Sigma=I$, $J$ *is* the ordinary objective.
